## Selection and grouping

Identify the recipient and purpose during reading time. Select the facts the recipient needs to understand the situation and act. Group related information into clear paragraphs. Retain chronology where it helps explain a change in condition; do not simply copy every dated entry. Social history belongs when it affects the recipient's understanding or care arrangements.

Apply to an AMR task: write the reader and purpose first, then sort the notes into essential, useful context and unnecessary for this reader. Check that important facts have not been omitted or changed.

## Formality and abbreviations

Turn case-note fragments into complete, accurate sentences. Use a professional tone and vocabulary appropriate to the recipient. The report's expansion examples include:

- mane → in the morning
- b.d. → twice daily
- t.d.s. → three times daily
- hx → history
- BP → blood pressure
- PR → pulse rate, when this is the meaning in the supplied context

These are language examples, not prescribing instructions. Do not guess an ambiguous abbreviation. The original report says to expand all acronyms; the applied checklist instead asks whether the intended reader will understand them.

## Linking ideas

The original report calls “so, because, moreover, besides, furthermore, thus, hence” banned connectors. No official ban has been verified. Choose a connector that accurately expresses cause, contrast or sequence, and remove it if it adds nothing. Do not replace clear language with a more formal word solely because it appears on a list.

## Review checklist

1. Is the purpose clear at the start and the requested action explicit?
2. Have I included the accurate information this reader needs?
3. Have I removed irrelevant details and repetition?
4. Are paragraphs logical and important details easy to find?
5. Is the language professional and understandable to this recipient?
6. Have I checked grammar, spelling, punctuation, dates and names?
